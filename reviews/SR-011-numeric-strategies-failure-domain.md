# Failure-domain review of the numeric/state strategy slice

## Summary

Reviewed `agent-e/codegen-3-numeric-strategies-spec` at `6f73e2d` against base `240fad8`. For each
requirement, the review listed how it can fail and checked that each failure has a detected,
reported outcome and a test. It checked the text against:

- base `src/strategy.rs`, `src/harness.rs` and `src/oracle.rs`;
- `quire-contract-ir` at the pinned `04eb6f8` (`check_compare`, `check_value_reference`,
  `observation_allowed`, `ClauseKind::accepts`, `IntegerLiteral` checking, `TypedExpression`);
- `quire-contract-runtime` at `8a4d02b` (`CampaignCounts`);
- `proptest` 1.5.0 (`Union` and `sample::select` shrinking).

It does not repeat the scope-boundary findings in REV-022 (SR-013). Where a failure mode overlaps
one of those findings, the row cites it.

What holds up:

- Admission is decided before rendering (FR-008-CON-2).
- Literal edges and domain edges use checked arithmetic.
- An empty population is refused, not generated silently.
- Zero-attempt rates return `None`.
- Extreme domains have ACs.

The failure modes that escape fall into five groups:

1. **Post values are subject outputs.** A generated `Post` value, and the tag computed from it,
   cannot predict the verdict once a subject produces the post state. As written, TC-020's
   preserving subject would fail its own campaign. Post-anchored cases also ignore the operation's
   preconditions.
2. **Read identity and anchors are underspecified.** Repeated reads (`x == x`), `Current` aliasing
   `Pre`/`Post`, and `Assertion` clauses at a `Pre` anchor can each produce impossible populations
   or verdict mappings that are wrong.
3. **Arithmetic at the extremes.** "Checked `i64`" cannot compute domain widths wider than
   `i64::MAX`. The stated fallback, shrinking the interval, would drop values silently. The
   exhaustive census uses domain pairs that the public IR cannot build, because a `Compare` requires
   both operands to have the identical `IntegerType`.
4. **Shrinking and accounting evidence has gaps.** proptest's `Union` and `select` shrink across
   tags by default, and no AC walks the `Broad` or `Boundary` shrink trees. `out_of_domain` sits
   outside the runtime's `attempted`.
5. **Strategy/oracle agreement is only a corpus subset check.** At base, the oracle refuses every
   `Compare`. No AC requires the tags to equal the generated oracle's result.

| FND-20001 | high | **Generated `Post` values conflict with subject-driven campaigns.** FR-009 generates the `Post` read and tags each case from it. FR-011 maps `Holds` to `Passed` and `Violated` to `FailedPostcondition`. But the base harness takes `post` from the subject (`invoke(input, state)` mutates the state), so the generated post value is overwritten and its tag predicts nothing. TC-020 step 1 contradicts itself: with a version-preserving subject, every `Violated` case (generated post ≠ pre) yields `Passed`, which is a mismatch, so "the preserving subject passes" cannot hold. **Fix:** specify two modes. (a) Oracle-conformance mode feeds generated (pre, post) snapshot pairs straight to the oracle or runtime with no subject, and tags predict verdicts. (b) Subject mode generates only `Input`, `Current` and `Pre` reads; a `Post`-anchored clause's verdict is observed, not predicted, so the campaign cannot fail on tag mismatch. Rewrite FR-011-AC-1 and TC-020 step 1 per mode. | FR-009 Behavior, FR-011 Behavior and AC-1, TC-020 step 1 and Expected Results, `src/harness.rs` facade/adapter | wrong-requirement |
| FND-20002 | medium | **Post-anchored populations ignore the operation's preconditions.** A `Post` clause's `Holds` cases are built from that one clause only. If the operation also has a `Pre`-anchored precondition (for example `version < 1000` on an increment), some cases return `RejectedPrecondition`. FR-011 does not say whether that is `ExpectationMismatch`, a rejected count, or an accepted verdict, so a correct subject can fail the campaign. **Fix:** either compose the operation's admitted preconditions into Post-anchored populations, or state that `RejectedPrecondition` on a Post-anchored case is counted in `rejected` and is never a mismatch. Add a TC-020 fixture with a narrowing precondition. | FR-011 Behavior (Post anchor), FR-009 Inputs, TC-020 | missing-requirement |
| FND-20003 | medium | **The verdict mapping is keyed on anchor, not clause kind, and the anchor is checked too late.** `ClauseKind::accepts` allows an `Assertion` at any anchor, so a `Pre`-anchored assertion would map `Violated` to `RejectedPrecondition`, reporting an assertion failure as a precondition rejection. `Clause.anchor` is also an `Option`, and FR-011 does not say what happens when it is absent. `UnsupportedAnchor` is decided in FR-011 (the runner), not in FR-008's admission list, so a `Handler`/`Initialization` clause can pass admission and census before being refused, which FR-008-CON-2 forbids. **Fix:** move the anchor check into FR-008 admission. Admit only `Precondition` at `Pre` and `Postcondition` at `Post`, and refuse every other kind/anchor pair, including an absent anchor, with `UnsupportedAnchor` or a new `UnsupportedClauseKind`. Add an `Assertion`-at-`Pre` fixture to TC-017. | FR-008 Behavior, FR-011 Behavior and AC-5, interface-001 `bound_strategy_slice.verdict_mapping`, IR `ClauseKind::accepts` | missing-requirement |
| FND-20004 | medium | **"No partial bundle" has evidence for only some refusals.** FR-008-AC-3 and TC-017 assert "no bundle" for FR-008's codes. No AC or TC asserts that nothing is emitted for `EmptyPopulation` (FR-009-AC-4), `UnsupportedCampaignConstraint` (FR-010-AC-4) or `UnsupportedAnchor` (FR-011-AC-5), and these are decided after admission, where a partial render is most likely. | FND-20005 | medium | **The identity of a generated read is undefined.** FR-008 says "one `i64` per generated read" and correlates only the `Pre`/`Post` reads of one `State` declaration. Three cases are left open. (a) The same (declaration, observation) on both sides (`x == x`, `amount < amount`) would become two independent values, so `Violating` would generate x ≠ x, which cannot happen at runtime. (b) IR allows `Current` alongside `Pre` at a `Pre` anchor and alongside `Post` at a `Post` anchor, and `Current` refers to the same runtime value as that observation. Treating them as distinct produces impossible cases. (c) FR-013's (name, observation) fields would collide or alias. **Fix:** key each generated value by (declaration, effective observation), where `Current` resolves to the anchor's observation. Say that operands sharing a key are one value, so the relation is decided statically and `EmptyPopulation` applies. Add `x == x` and `current == pre` fixtures to TC-017 and TC-018. | FR-008 Outputs and Behavior, FR-009 Behavior, FR-013 Outputs, IR `observation_allowed` | missing-requirement |
| FND-20006 | medium | **The heterogeneous-domain cases cannot come from checked IR.** `check_compare` refuses operands whose `ValueType` differs, and `IntegerType` carries its minimum, maximum, domain and overflow policy. So both reads, and any literal, always share one domain, and `IntegerLiteral` checking puts the literal inside it. FR-009's "intersection of both domains", FR-010's "nearest in-domain value of the second read", and TC-018 step 3's cross pairs (for example [0,0] against [2,5]) describe inputs that no `BoundPackage` can hold. Building them contradicts FR-008-CON-1 and TC-017 step 1 ("through the public IR API"). **Fix:** state the IR invariant that operands share an identical `IntegerType` and literals lie inside it. Restrict the public-API census to same-domain pairs, including 1-member and extreme domains. If cross-domain coverage is still wanted, name an internal interval-engine seam and test it there, labelled as not reachable from IR. | FR-009 Behavior and AC-2, FR-010 Behavior, TC-018 step 3, FR-008-CON-1, IR `check_compare`, `IntegerLiteral` check | wrong-requirement |
| FND-20007 | medium | **Checked `i64` arithmetic cannot represent wide domains, and the stated fallback drops values.** The domain width (`max - min`) and FR-009's `NotEqual` "reduced size" index overflow `i64` for any domain wider than `i64::MAX` (for example `i64::MIN..=0`, or the full range). The outcome of a failed checked operation is unspecified, and "shrink the interval rather than wrap" would drop values silently, contradicting FR-009-AC-2's "none missing" and FR-010's never-drop rule. The extremes in TC-018 are only 2-member domains and are enumerated rather than compiled. So the partner computations the generated Rust performs at run time (`first ± 1`), and the emission of `i64::MIN` literals, have no evidence. **Fix:** compute widths and indices in `i128`/`u128`. Define that an interval endpoint is unrepresentable only when that side is empty. Require generated code to precompute partner bounds or use checked operations, and to emit `i64::MIN`/`i64::MAX` as path tokens. Add full-range and half-range `NotEqual`/`Less` fixtures that compile and run as generated Rust. | FR-009 Behavior and AC-5, FR-010-AC-3, TC-018 step 3, NFR-004 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-20008 | medium | **The default proptest combinators break tag-preserving shrinking, and no test covers them.** In proptest 1.5.0, `Union::simplify` switches to an earlier alternative (`pick` goes down toward `min_pick`), so a `prop_oneof` `Broad` built as `[Satisfying, Violating]` shrinks a `Violated` case into the `Holds` branch. `sample::select` shrinks by binary search on the index toward 0 across the whole census, crossing tags. FR-010's Outputs prescribe exactly one `select` over the census. FR-012 requires per-branch and same-tag shrinking, but FR-012-AC-1/AC-2 and TC-021 walk only `Satisfying` and `Violating` trees. **Fix:** change FR-010 Outputs to one `select` per tag inside a union that shrinks within its branch (or a custom value tree). | FND-20009 | medium | **Out-of-domain accounting and the consumer admission hook are underspecified.** Runtime `CampaignCounts::total()` is accepted + rejected + discarded, and the generated summary sets `attempted` from it. An `out_of_domain` count kept outside it (see FND-22002) therefore changes what the rates mean: a `Boundary` campaign of only out-of-domain cases reports `attempted == 0`, and the rates return `None` even though cases ran. Several things are unstated: whether shrink replays of out-of-domain cases are counted; the consumer domain-admission hook's signature, purity and determinism; what happens if it panics or is never supplied; and whether `ExpectationMismatch` is a new outcome variant, since base has only `Failed`. The runner also counts `discarded` from a caller-supplied prior report, so FR-011's zero-ceiling rule fails a constructive campaign because of the caller's earlier discards. **Fix:** state the `attempted` denominator explicitly, with or without out-of-domain cases. Give the admission hook a typed signature in interface-001 with a pure/deterministic requirement and panic-as-campaign-failure. Name the outcome variant. | FND-20010 | medium | **Strategy/oracle agreement is only a subset check against an oracle that does not exist at base.** Base `generate_boolean_oracle` refuses `Compare` as `UnsupportedExpression`, so today FR-008-AC-5 either passes vacuously or would force strategy generation to refuse everything.
